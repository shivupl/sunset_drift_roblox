# Drift-by-drivetrain and exhaust-flutter research

## 1. Real drift technique by drivetrain

### RWD
- **Power-over**: full throttle at corner entry/exit breaks rear traction and holds the car sideways; throttle is the "hold" input — more gas = more angle, lifting tightens the car back toward straight ("lift to catch"). [Car Throttle: 5 Ways To Initiate A Drift](https://www.carthrottle.com/news/5-ways-initiate-drift), [SLR Speed guide](https://slrspeed.com/blogs/news/how-to-drift-a-car-the-ultimate-guide-to-mastering-drifting)
- **Clutch-kick**: clutch in during a drift, rev up, then dump the clutch — the sudden driveline jolt snaps the rear loose again, used specifically to *add angle* when the car is about to straighten. [Car Throttle](https://www.carthrottle.com/news/5-ways-initiate-drift)
- **Scandinavian flick / pendulum**: steer away from the turn first, then snap back into it; the reversed weight transfer flicks the rear out before the corner, used in rally on loose surfaces and adapted to drift entries. [Wikipedia: Scandinavian flick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_flick)
- **Handbrake entry**: e-brake locks the rears instantly, car rotates, driver then balances on throttle/counter-steer through the rest of the corner — the simplest, least speed-dependent entry.
- **Angle/speed numbers**: competitive drift slip angles run **45–60°**; steering lock on dedicated drift cars is **70–80°** vs **35–45°** stock. Entry speeds at competition vary **60–100 mph**, settling to roughly **50–60 mph** through most of the course; top pro-level peak speeds reach **~93 mph (150 km/h)**. [SLR Speed – angle kit guide](https://slrspeed.com/blogs/news/best-angle-kit-steering-upgrade-guide-slr), [Driftworks forum – average speeds](https://www.driftworks.com/forum/threads/average-drifting-speeds.217978/)
- **Lift & counter-steer**: lifting off throttle transfers weight forward, rear regains grip and the car snaps straight — the main way RWD drifts are "caught"; counter-steer (wheel turned opposite the rear's slide) is held throughout to aim the nose.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Drifting (motorsport)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drifting_(motorsport)), [Driftworks forum thread "I have learned lol"](https://www.driftworks.com/forum/threads/i-have-learned-lol.91684/), [Steam Guide: Drifting Techs](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3091941157)

### FWD
- **Why throttle straightens the car**: the driven front tyres both steer *and* pull — gas pulls the car in the direction the front wheels are pointed, so throttle drags the nose (and the whole car) back into line instead of holding a slide. This is the opposite of RWD, where throttle spins the rear and *extends* the slide.
- **Lift-off oversteer**: carry speed near the grip limit into a corner, then suddenly lift the throttle completely. The abrupt deceleration shifts weight forward off the rear axle, cutting rear grip and letting the back end swing out — a pure weight-transfer technique needing no handbrake. [Wikipedia: Lift-off oversteer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift-off_oversteer), [The Drive: Can You Drift a FWD Car?](https://www.thedrive.com/news/27898/can-you-drift-a-front-wheel-drive-car)
- **Handbrake drift**: e-brake breaks rear traction to start the rotation; handbrake duration controls slide length since there's no throttle input sustaining it.
- **Left-foot braking / reverse entry**: left-foot brake trail used to keep the front loaded while still on throttle; "reverse entry" (backing into a slide, Scandinavian-flick style — counter-steer away, then snap back) is the rally-derived FWD/AWD entry.
- **Character of an FWD drift**: shorter, "transitional" slides/"snap drifts" rather than sustained ones — because there's no throttle input that holds the angle, the slide inherently decays and the car straightens itself out once you're back on the gas; FWD drifting needs more entry momentum since you can't rebuild angle mid-corner the way RWD can. [Modded.com: Can You Drift With a FWD?](https://modded.com/cars/drift-with-a-fwd/), [Engineer Fix: How to Drift a FWD Car](https://engineerfix.com/how-to-drift-a-fwd-car-techniques-preparation/)

### AWD / 4WD
- **Four-wheel drift / rally power slide**: because all four tyres are driven, breaking rear grip takes more deliberate initiation (handbrake, Scandi flick, big initial throttle/brake transfer) — AWD doesn't "fall into" a slide the way RWD does.
- **Scandinavian flick**: the archetypal AWD/rally entry — counter-steer away then flick back in, using inertia to swing the tail out, popularized by Nordic rally drivers from the 1960s. [Wikipedia: Scandinavian flick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_flick)
- **Torque split and "drift mode"**: stock AWD hot hatches bias torque rearward to make sliding possible/fun.
  - **Ford Focus RS**: factory Drift Mode sends up to **70% of torque to the rear axle**, loosens stability control and firms dampers. [evo: How the Focus RS drift mode works](https://www.evo.co.uk/ford/focus-rs/17242/wonder-how-the-ford-focus-rs-drift-mode-works-heres-the-answer)
  - **Audi RS3** (Torque Splitter rear axle): multi-plate clutches can send up to **100% of drive to either rear wheel** in Torque Rear/RS Performance mode. [Carsguide](https://www.carsguide.com.au/car-news/2022-audi-rs3-puts-mercedes-amg-a45-s-on-notice-drift-mode-engaged-as-new-awd-monster-uses)
  - **Mercedes-AMG A45 S**: active torque-vectoring AWD, 416 bhp, similarly rear-biasable. [evo: RS3 v GR Yaris v A45 S](https://www.evo.co.uk/audi/rs3/207729/audi-rs3-v-toyota-gr-yaris-v-mercedes-amg-a45-s-which-is-the-best-awd-hyper-hatch)
  - **Toyota GR Yaris**: no active vectoring — passive Torsen diffs front and rear that bias torque to whichever wheel still has grip, so its "drift" behavior is more old-school 4WD power slide than an electronically gamed one. [gr-yaris.co.uk](https://www.gr-yaris.co.uk/threads/gr-yaris-awd-system-explained.916/)
  - General split context: 50:50 is neutral/stable; rear-biased 30:70 (or higher) lets the back end step out like RWD while the front keeps pulling the car forward out of the slide.
- **What differs physically**: in any drift, the axle whose tyres have exceeded their lateral-grip limit (are "spinning"/slipping sideways) is the one that's sliding. RWD spins the rear only, so the rear slides while the front (steered, not driven) still has grip to aim with. FWD's driven front wheels pull the car in the direction they're pointed, which both induces the lift-off slide at the limit and kills it the moment you reapply throttle. AWD, with power to both axles, needs an initial bias/traction-break to get the rear sliding but once in the slide the front axle's drive pulls the car forward/straight even as the rear is sliding, letting the car make forward progress (not just rotate) through the slide — this is why AWD drifts are described as lower-angle, higher-forward-speed, and self-straightening on power compared to RWD's sustained, higher-angle slide that only straightens on lift. [slrspeed.com: Is AWD Better for Drifting?](https://slrspeed.com/blogs/news/is-awd-better-for-drifting-exploring-the-pros-and-cons), [GTPlanet: AWD vs RWD](https://www.gtplanet.net/forum/threads/awd-vs-rwd.47085/), [Forza forums: Drift in AWD vs RWD](https://forums.forza.net/t/drift-in-awd-vs-rwd/89524)

## 2. Saudi hajwala/tafheet specifics
- **Origin**: subculture believed to date to the **late 1970s** in Saudi Arabia, centered on Riyadh and Al-Qassim Province (some crossover activity in UAE). [Wikipedia: Tafheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafheet)
- **Cars and drivetrain**:
  - **FWD sedans (most common)**: Toyota Camry, Avalon; Honda Accord; Nissan Altima/Maxima; Hyundai Sonata/Elantra/Accent; Kia Optima/Cadenza; Chevrolet Cruze/Malibu — cheap, disposable, often not personally owned (rented or "borrowed").
  - **RWD**: Chevrolet Caprice, Chevrolet Lumina, Dodge Charger; Toyota Hilux can be run in RWD mode.
  - **SUVs/trucks**: Toyota Land Cruiser (older and modern) used for sideways passing/"tatweef."
  - Occasionally high-end cars (Rolls-Royce, Ferrari, Nissan GT-R) but these are rare relative to ordinary sedans.
  - [Wikipedia: Tafheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafheet), [ArabAmerica: Tafheet — Saudi Arabia's Drifting Culture](https://www.arabamerica.com/tafheet-saudi-arabias-drifting-culture/), [ArabWheels: 8 Cars That Shaped Saudi Motoring Culture](https://www.arabwheels.sa/blog/en/saudi-motoring-culture-iconic-cars/)
- **Speeds**: drivers swing the car side-to-side ("hajwala") at roughly **160–260 km/h (100–160 mph)** on open highway.
- **Named maneuvers** (Wikipedia/Grokipedia):
  - **Tanteel**: repeated powerslides corrected with opposite-lock steering, over and over.
  - **Aksiyat**: full 180–360° rotations.
  - **Harakat Al-Mawt** ("movement of death"): holding a powerslide while still driving forward, without steering correction — i.e., committing to the slide.
  - **Tatweef**: passing another car while sideways, at very high speed, on a public highway — the signature "overtake while drifting" move, including by 4WD trucks/SUVs sliding past traffic.
- **Mechanism for FWD cars**: with no driven rear axle to spin up, the technique is handbrake + aggressive opposing steering to break rear traction at speed and then keep steering the car side to side; the car's own momentum/speed sustains the slide rather than sustained rear-wheel power, matching the real-world FWD drift behavior described in section 1 (needs momentum, ends/straightens once power is reapplied).
- **Recent legitimization**: the Saudi Automobile and Motorcycle Federation (SAMF) issued sporting regulations in **June 2026**, holding the first sanctioned hajwala event on **June 26, 2026** in Riyadh. [Saudi Times: From Drifting to Discipline](https://sauditimes.org/insights/society/from-drifting-to-discipline-the-changing-face-of-saudi-car-culture/)

## 3. Exhaust flames in drift videos
Mechanisms, roughly in increasing drama:
- **Overrun/lift-off backfire**: on throttle lift, if the ECU doesn't cut fuel on a closed throttle, unburned fuel reaches the hot exhaust/turbo and ignites — a single pop/flash each time the driver lifts. [Grassroots Motorsports forum](https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/grm/why-do-race-cars-spit-fire/45729/page1/)
- **Rev-limiter bounce ("limiter flames")**: a hard-cut limiter cuts fuel/spark at the set RPM; if throttle stays open, RPM sags, the cut releases, RPM climbs back to the limit, and it cuts again — a rapid on/off cycle. Fuel that was still flowing during the cut ignites in the exhaust when spark returns, giving a flame that pulses in time with each cut/recover cycle. A longer "hysteresis" (RPM drop before re-enable) gives a slower, choppier bounce; racers use very short hysteresis to minimize power loss, which also makes a faster flicker. [Wikipedia: Rev limiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rev_limiter), [LS1Tech forum: Why do rev limiters bounce?](https://ls1tech.com/forums/general-lsx-automobile-discussion/1393315-why-do-rev-limiters-bounce.html)
- **Anti-lag (ALS)**: deliberately retards ignition timing (~30° retard) and over-fuels so combustion finishes burning past the exhaust port/into the manifold, keeping the turbo spooled off-throttle; this produces repeated small explosions — described as "tens of explosions" over a few seconds, giving the characteristic rapid "bang-bang-bang" and continuous flame/sparks out the exhaust. No source gave an exact Hz figure, but "tens of bangs in a few seconds" implies roughly **5–15 Hz** during active anti-lag. [Driver61: Anti-Lag Explained](https://driver61.com/motorsport-engineering/anti-lag-rallying-turbo-boost/), [autoevolution: How Anti-Lag Systems Work](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/how-anti-lag-systems-work-25222.html)
- **Two-step launch control**: a secondary, lower launch-rev-limiter used off the line (and sometimes "limiter-bashed" for show) that produces the same bounce-flame effect at a chosen RPM, often tuned specifically to look/sound good when "bashing the limiter." [forum.cfx.re: Tuner Exhaust (Anti-lag, 2Step & Backfire)](https://forum.cfx.re/t/free-github-tuner-exhaust-anti-lag-2step-backfire/5093731)
- **Feathering the throttle mid-drift**: rapid stabs on/off the gas to manage angle and speed through a sustained slide; this is heard as the exhaust note "rising and falling rapidly," and each stab that closes the throttle can produce its own small overrun pop — so feathering is itself a flame-rhythm source, separate from a limiter. [S-Chassis.com: Throttle Manipulation and Control](https://www.s-chassis.com/forums/road-race-autox-45/lesson-5-throttle-manipulation-control-38527/)
- **Clutch kick**: a single sharp flame/bang can accompany the rev-flare-and-dump of a clutch kick, similar in look to one limiter-bounce cycle but driver-timed rather than periodic.
- **Visual character** (synthesized from the above, no single authoritative source gives a flame-length/color spec): overrun pops read as **brief single flashes** (one frame or two); rev-limiter/anti-lag flame reads as a **flickering, pulsing "tongue"** that stays lit in bursts timed to the cut-recover cycle rather than one continuous flame, typically **orange-to-blue** at the hottest/leanest moments, **longer and bluer** under anti-lag (sustained, high-pressure combustion) and **shorter, oranger, more sporadic** under simple overrun/limiter bounce.

## 4. How driving games present drivetrain and drift presets
- **Forza Horizon (6/5/4)**: drifting is tuned almost exclusively around **RWD or AWD-converted-to-rear-biased**; FWD cars must first be swapped to AWD, then tuned with power sent almost entirely to the rear (RWD drift diff: ~95–100% accel lock / 10–40% decel lock) to behave like RWD. AWD with a rear bias of roughly 75–100% is called the "most forgiving" drift setup because the front keeps enough pull to initiate/point the slide. Other levers: drift suspension/ARBs, max front negative camber (−5°), rear tire pressure dropped for less grip, toe settings. [forzafire.com: Drivetrain Tuning Guide](https://www.forzafire.com/guides/forza-horizon-6-drivetrain-tuning-guide), [Steam discussion: RWD Drift Tuning Guide](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2483190/discussions/4/569288789836623491/)
- **CarX Drift Racing Online**: exposes a **drift assist** toggle (most players keep it on; off is "twitchy and uncontrollable"), **max steering angle** (players push it to the limit), **Ackermann angle** (set to 0%), and camber (front ~−3°, rear ~−4°) as the core drift-feel knobs; wheel users pick 360°/540° rotation to match hardware. [racinggames.gg: Settings, tips and tricks](https://racinggames.gg/article/carx-drift-racing-online-settings-tips-and-tricks), [Steam guide: Adjusting your car for drifting](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1104486321)
- **NFS Heat / Unbound**: Heat exposes a binary choice — **"Brake to Drift"** vs **"Gas to Drift"** — i.e. which pedal tap initiates a slide mid-corner. Unbound replaces/extends this with a continuous **handling slider** (grip ↔ drift) that drivetrain/chassis parts shift, plus a separate **"Drift entry"** toggle controlling whether lifting-and-reapplying throttle (or tapping brakes) triggers a drift; both assist options can be disabled to force handbrake-only drift initiation. [gamepressure.com: Handling tuning and drifting](https://www.gamepressure.com/need-for-speed-unbound/handling-tuning-and-drifting/zd10705)
- **Assetto Corsa** (community drift mods, since AC itself has no native drift mode): nearly all drift builds are RWD, including AWD chassis with front driveshafts removed. Knobs: diff power (higher = more eager to gain angle, lower if uncontrollable), diff coast (lower = more eager to switch directions when lifting, higher = more stable), diff preload (higher = more resistant to direction change), steering lock (45–55° minimum vs ~45° stock), negative camber (−2° to −4°), tyre compound (street vs drift-specific). Reference chassis: Nissan S13/S14/S15, 180SX, Skyline R32–34, AE86, Mark II, Supra MKIV, RX-7 FD — virtually all classic RWD JDM. [overtake.gg: Assetto Corsa Drift Setup Guide](https://www.overtake.gg/news/assetto-corsa-drift-setup-guide-beginners-edition.2203/), [Steam guide: basic drift setup guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2984712533)
- **Torque Drift / Torque Drift 2**: built specifically around RWD (or 4WD cars raced as RWD); setup focus is diff and suspension; limited public detail beyond general RWD-drift tuning community knowledge was found — treat as thin evidence. [Steam guide: Drift car setup [ENG]](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2761221841)
- **Roblox drift games**:
  - **Midnight Racing: Tokyo**: no dedicated drift mode, but tuning exposes **max steering angle** (players set to max) and an **"aggro"/countersteer-assist** value that controls how much the car self-corrects/balances mid-drift without player steering input — softer aggro at max steering angle is the preferred "easy big angle" combo. [gameplay.tips: Keyboard Drift Tuning Guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/midnight-racing-tokyo-keyboard-drift-tuning-guide.html)
  - **Driving Empire**: handbrake (Space/B) + steer to break traction, tap-and-release handbrake then reapply throttle to link/extend a slide — closely mirrors the real RWD handbrake-entry-then-throttle-hold pattern. [progameguides.com: How to drift in Driving Empire](https://progameguides.com/roblox/how-to-drift-in-driving-empire-roblox/)
  - Both games' player-facing knobs are simple (steering angle, a single "how much does the car help me" slider, handbrake timing) rather than exposing diff/torque-split detail — suggesting Roblox audiences respond to a small number of legible sliders rather than sim-depth parameters.

## Implications for a game

**RWD — three drift styles**
1. **Power-over**: player holds throttle through the slide; throttle = angle (more throttle holds/extends angle), lift = car snaps straight. High-angle, speed-bleeding, classic "tandem" look.
2. **Clutch-kick**: a discrete player action (tap a "kick" input) mid-drift that snaps the rear loose again for a burst of extra angle at a one-time speed cost — good as a skill-timing mechanic layered on top of power-over.
3. **Handbrake entry / Scandi flick**: a quick-start input (handbrake tap, or counter-steer-then-flick) that gets the car sideways instantly at corner entry, then hands off to throttle-hold for the rest of the slide — this is the "easy initiation" style for players who don't want to manage weight transfer.

**FWD — two to three drift styles**
1. **Handbrake throw** (matches Saudi hajwala FWD behavior directly): e-brake snap breaks rear traction at speed; the car then free-slides on momentum alone (no throttle sustaining it), straightening on its own once the driver reapplies gas — shorter, momentum-based, "thrown" rather than "held."
2. **Lift-off oversteer**: player lifts fully off throttle approaching a corner at speed; weight transfer forward unloads the rear and kicks it out — pure finesse/weight-transfer style, no handbrake, rewards entry speed and timing over sustained throttle control.
3. (Optional) **Reverse entry / flick**: counter-steer-away-then-snap, rally-style, for a more advanced FWD option that mimics the Scandinavian flick without a driven rear axle.
All FWD styles should share the trait that throttle pulls the nose straight — so "holding" an FWD drift under power should be hard/impossible by design, forcing shorter slides than RWD.

**AWD/4WD — two to three drift styles**
1. **Rally/Scandi power slide**: counter-steer flick to break the rear loose, then power through — lower angle than RWD, but the car keeps making strong forward progress through the slide (front axle still pulling), good for a "fast and flowing" rather than "sideways and showy" feel.
2. **Rear-biased drift mode** (Focus RS/A45/RS3-style): a selectable torque-split state (e.g., 30:70 or more rearward) that makes the AWD car behave closer to RWD — easier to hold higher angle, at the cost of the AWD's natural straight-line pull-out. This maps well to an in-game toggle/upgrade the player unlocks, distinct from the default balanced split.
3. (Optional) **Tatweef/overtake slide**: an AWD/4WD truck-flavored style tuned for sustained, lower-angle, high-forward-speed sliding past other traffic — fits the Saudi truck ("tatweef") reference directly and differentiates big 4x4s from the hot-hatch "drift mode" style.

**Exhaust flutter — top 2–3 traits to nail**
1. **Rhythm tied to a real cause, not random flicker**: tie flame pulses to discrete in-fiction events — throttle-lift pops (single flash), feathering stabs (flash per stab, player-paced), and a rev-limiter/anti-lag "hold" state during a sustained drift (a fast, periodic bounce, roughly 5–15 Hz, i.e. several flickers per second) — rather than one continuous fire or pure noise-driven flicker.
2. **Visual distinction by cause**: short single flash for overrun/lift-off pops; a pulsing, sustained "tongue" that flutters in place for limiter-bounce/anti-lag holds (longer duration, more blue-white at the hottest moments, orange at the edges/cooling); a one-off bright flash+bang for clutch-kick, timed to the player's kick input.
3. **Gate it to drivetrain/style**: RWD power-over and clutch-kick are the natural home for the showiest, most sustained flutter (throttle stays open through the whole slide); FWD styles should flutter less/shorter since the throttle is mostly closed or being lifted (overrun pops dominate, not sustained limiter bounce); AWD rear-biased drift mode sits in between — frequent enough to read as "performance mode" but not as constant as RWD's held-throttle slide.
